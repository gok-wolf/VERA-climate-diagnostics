## Read outputs jointly

No single VERA layer provides a complete ecological interpretation. Magnitude,
identity, direction, climatic proximity and covariance-aware departure answer
different questions.

## Mean and maximum VRS

Mean VRS summarises cumulative restriction. Maximum VRS reports the strongest
single predictor-specific signal. Neither establishes causality.

## Delta VRS

Delta VRS records separation between the two leading signals. Small values
indicate weak resolution; zero indicates a shared maximum.

## VPI

Higher VPI values indicate greater proximity to the occupied climatic
reference. VPI is not habitat suitability or occurrence probability.

## Primary and Unique Primary Stressors

Primary Stressor provides a deterministic leading-predictor label. Unique
Primary retains the identity only where one predictor has a strict maximum.
Unique means resolved dominance, not suitable habitat or causal limitation.

## TooHigh and TooLow Stressors

These identify the strongest signals above and below predictor-specific
occupied reference centres and support directional ecological hypotheses.

## Mahalanobis tiers and add-ons

Mahalanobis tiers describe covariance-aware climatic departure. Add-on 1
compares dominant and cumulative direction; Add-on 2 localises agreement and
disagreement between VRS and Mahalanobis landscape rankings.
